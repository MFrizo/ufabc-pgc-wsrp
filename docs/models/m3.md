# M3 — Pareto front of the agency and the brokers

- **Builder:** `build_model_m3` in `src/models/model_3.py`
- **Front:** `pareto_front` in the same module
- **Role:** the plans that are not worse for one side without being better for the other
- **Instance settings:** M1's, plus `days_off = 1`. Every visit is booked: `--fixed-ratio` is ignored (`STRICT_TIME_MODELS`)

M1 and M2 do not share a feasible set, so a pair of their solutions is not a Pareto front. A route that is legal in one can be illegal in the other. M3 keeps the rules neither side gives up, and turns each side's preferences into an objective.

The rules that stay as constraints are M1's: each visit served once, an already-assigned visit done by its broker, the soft windows, the 12-hour day, and a lunch break. The agency's objective is M1's. The brokers' objective is the M2 rules that M1 does not impose: minutes of the route outside the broker's own shifts, a day worked on his day off, and the extra trip to the lunch spot he picked.

`main.py --model m3` does not return one plan. It returns the front.

## Sets and parameters

The sets, the arc distances and the travel times are [M1](m1.md)'s. Two lists come from the generator and M1 does not read them:

| Symbol | Pyomo | Data key | Definition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| — | `model.K_on`, `model.K_off` | `shifts` | Brokers who have shifts, and brokers whose list is empty |
| $E_{ks}, L_{ks}$ | — | `shifts` | Start and end of shift $s$ of broker $k$ |
| — | `model.fleet_cap` | the argument `fleet_cap` | Most brokers who may work in this solve. One solve of the front uses each value from 1 to $\|K\|$ |

## Decision variables

M1's variables, and:

| Symbol | Pyomo | Domain | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $o_{ks}$ | `model.overlap` | $\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ | Minutes of broker $k$'s route that fall inside shift $s$ |
| $u_k$ | `model.outside` | $\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ | Minutes of that route outside all of his shifts |
| $z_{ijk}$ | `model.z` | $\{0,1\}$ | $1$ if broker $k$'s lunch sits between nodes $i$ and $j$ |

## Objectives

**Agency, $f_1$.** The [M1](m1.md) objective: brokers who work, then travel time, delay and overtime. It is not minimized inside one solve. It is read off each solved plan, and the number of brokers is what the front constrains.

**Brokers, $f_2$** (`model.broker_cost`), in minutes:

$$
f_2 = \sum_{k \in K_{\text{on}}} u_k + J_{\max} \sum_{k \in K_{\text{off}}} y_k + \sum_{(i,j,k)} \frac{\max(0,\ \ell_{ijk})}{v}\, z_{ijk}
$$

$J_{\max} = 720$ is the longest day, so a broker who works on his day off costs as much as being out for that whole day. $\ell_{ijk}$ is the extra distance of going from $i$ to his lunch spot and on to $j$ instead of from $i$ to $j$, and $v = 2$ map units per minute is M1's speed. The virtual end of the day has no onward trip, so the extra distance is only the trip to the spot. The term is those extra minutes, not the raw distance to the spot.

M2's fuel bill is a different objective. M2 prices kilometres, and on M2 the shifts are hard constraints. The shifts, the day off and the chosen lunch spot are the M2 rules that M1 leaves free, so M3 scores them in minutes. A visit between the morning shift and the afternoon shift falls outside both. Counting only a start before the first shift and a finish after the last one would treat that visit as covered. $o_{ks}$ is the overlap of the route with shift $s$, and $u_k$ is the rest of the route.

One solve minimizes $f_2$. A tie-break on travel time is smaller than one minute of $f_2$, so it never buys a minute outside a shift.

## The front

The number of brokers takes few integer values, so one MILP per fleet size is the $\varepsilon$-constraint method (Mavrotas, 2009):

$$
\min f_2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad \sum_{k \in K} y_k \le m, \qquad m = 1, \dots, |K|
$$

A size the visits do not fit is skipped. A plan is dropped when another plan uses no more brokers and costs the brokers no more, and is strictly better on one of the two. What remains is the front, ordered by the number of brokers. It can be a single plan when the instance does not force a trade-off. That is a result, not a missing point.

A weighted sum of $f_1$ and $f_2$ is not used. On an integer front it only finds the convex hull (Mavrotas, 2009). AUGMECON is the usual form of the $\varepsilon$-constraint method. The balanced box method is an exact alternative for two objectives (Boland, Charkhgard and Savelsbergh, 2015). Surveys of multi-objective routing, and the closest applied front, are Jozefowiez, Semet and Talbi (2008) and Braekers, Hartl, Parragh and Tricoire (2016). The home-care paper is the model for the chapter: a front between operating cost and the inconvenience of the people being served. Each of these solves is about as hard as M1. Gurobi's `setObjectiveN` returns one lexicographic or blended point per run, not the front.

## Constraints

Constraints (1) to (9) of [M1](m1.md) are unchanged.

**(10) At most $m$ brokers** (`fleet_cap_constraint`):

$$
\sum_{k \in K} y_k \le m
$$

**(11) Overlap with one shift** (`overlap_length_constraint` and the constraints that follow it). For a broker who works, $o_{ks}$ is the length of the intersection of his route $[w_{d_k}, w_{f_k}]$ with shift $[E_{ks}, L_{ks}]$, and $u_k$ is whatever part of the route is left:

$$
u_k \ge w_{f_k} - w_{d_k} - \sum_{s} o_{ks} - M(1 - y_k) \qquad \forall k \in K_{\text{on}}
$$

**(12) Where the lunch sits** (`lunch_cover_constraint`, `lunch_predecessor_constraint`, `lunch_successor_constraint`):

$$
\sum_{i,j} z_{ijk} = y_k, \qquad
\sum_{j} z_{ijk} = x_{i,a_k,k}, \qquad
\sum_{i} z_{ijk} = x_{a_k,j,k}
$$

## Remarks

- **The ends of the front.** The smallest fleet is the agency's side of this model: fewer brokers, with M1's travel time, delay and overtime read off the plan. A larger fleet stays on the front only when it lowers $f_2$. Neither point has to equal the M1 solution or the M2 solution. M2 minimizes fuel on two hard shifts, and its longest day only breaks a fuel tie (Matl, Hartl and Vidal, 2018). That term stays inside M2. The citation is in [Capacity](../decisions/capacity.md).
- **The lunch trip is free in the clock and paid in $f_2$.** As in M1, the arcs through the virtual lunch node have no travel time. The extra minutes to the real lunch spot are only in the brokers' objective.
- **Picking one plan is a separate decision.** The front is the set of trade-offs. Three usual choices are the compromise point (closest to the ideal point, after normalizing), the knee (where a small loss on one side stops buying much on the other), and Nash bargaining (the product of each side's gain over its worst outcome). M3 returns the front and does not choose. A front of one plan is a valid result when the instance forces no trade-off.
